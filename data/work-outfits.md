_Built 2026-08-12 from wardrobe.json, revised 2026-08-24 after correcting the office read: it's genuinely relaxed/casual (devs in t-shirts, jeans, puffer vests, sneakers), not a stiff smart-casual bank floor. Max confirmed looking smart is still welcome, so this isn't "go full casual" — it's two honest registers instead of one over-dressed default: **Everyday** (what actually fits the room day to day) and **Sharp** (when you want to make an effort, still current rather than dated). ## What changed from the first pass (2026-08-12) and why
The original six were all tuned for a stiffer "bank smart-casual" read and defaulted to the smartest available option every time. That was based on an assumption, not on how Max actually dresses — corrected 2026-08-24 once he explained the real dress code and his actual habits (vest daily in winter, blazer whenever he wants a jacket, overcoat rarely because it's too formal). The five kept looks (#3–5, #8, #10) still hold up fine; #6, #7, #9 are new and use the outerwear correctly; #1–2 are new "actual daily default" looks that weren't represented before because the first pass assumed casual wasn't really an option here.

## The honest catch
**Still true:** brown shoes dominate (only two black options — Ecco sneaker and the ageing black Chelsea, which is a Replace verdict), pale trousers outnumber dark ones, and V-neck knits still can't be worn alone (no crew tees owned). **No longer a constraint:** formality headroom — the office doesn't ask for "smartest possible" every day, so looks #1, #2, #6 exist now as genuinely correct defaults, not downgrades.
